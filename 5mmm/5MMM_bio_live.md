## welcome to blitz magic!

yup, you heard me right. that **[terrible idea](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-_6jXkMqg9c&t=1s)** which clearly breaks under magic's priority system? **_we're doin it_**.

* **what** - timed games. 5 minutes each, 2 second increments. grab your [chess](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chess-clock-by-chess-com/id858039162) [clocks](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.chess.clock&hl=en-US&pli=1).
* **where** - March of the Machines cube draft - arguably one of the deepest, most interactive limited environments of all time.
* **why** - for vibes. just let your cards do the thinking. magic looks so different from up here.
* **how** - one simple, innocuous rules tweak!

> _> only the active player gets priority_


## here's how it works

1. Draft that cube as usual.
2. Before each match round, confirm that every match has a chess clock (app) ready. 
3. Make sure that everyone knows how to use their chess clock.
4. Shuffle up, choose a starting player & resolve mulligans.
5. On the starting player's signal, their opponent starts the clock for them, and the game begins.
6. Smack that clock after attacking, blocking, or ending your turn.
7. If your time runs out, you lose the game!

> 🌈**TMYK** - a chess clock is basically two timers and two buttons - one for each player. it sets a starting time (5:00) for each player, as well as an increment (0:00 - we don't use this). as you take your turn, your timer counts down. when you're done, tap your button to stop your timer, and your opponent's timer starts. after your opponent does the same, your timer resumes countdown where it left off. think MTGO match timers, but in paper!

okay, look. this might seem like a dramatic bastardization of magic's golden essence, but some of us just wanna fly, okay? a turn of bare-bones, draw-go magic takes **at least 16 priority passes**, and you will literally break your phone before finishing a game. magic deserves a visceral, kinesthetic counterpart and what we lose in strategic complexity, we make up for in ✨_speed_✨.

conveniently, now that "priority" is more or less obsolete, we can fill this void in our vocabulary with clock instead! you _have clock_ when your timer is actively counting down. you can also _receive clock_, _pass clock_, _gain clock_, _hold clock_ - it's quite nice! none of this affects priority, but boy is it fun to say🌸

## ground rules

to tame the ultimately frantic late-game blitz, let's set some expectations before we start. these eight (8) rules have catchy names so that you can scream them in the throes of battle. what's that, they sound like staff training at a fast food joint? yeah, for the same dang reason. (just please don't say "CAYGO" in this context, it's very confusing😅)

* **our game, our job** - you are both responsible for pointing out any changes needed to reflect a clear and accurate game-state on either side of the board.
* **my stuff, my job** - you are responsible for moving your physical stuff on the table. that includes moving cards and counters, tapping lands, updating your life total, etc.
* **my clock, my pace** - don't wait for your opponent while you're on the clock. as long as you've communicated any changes needed to their stuff, proceed as though it has been fixed.
* **clean as you go** - don't wait to tidy up! update your stuff as your opponent plays. it saves you time playing catch-up when clock is passed to you!
* **talk it out** - announce all your actions & triggers clearly, just like in "real magic". passing priority is the exception. heck priority! 🏴‍☠️
* **skip blocks** - if you're not attacking, blocks are skipped. you retain clock straight through your combat step.
* **resolve-update-announce** (RUA /ruːˈæ/) - always complete these steps before passing clock, as well as before taking your first game action after gaining clock (ie. before untapping or blocking).
  1. **resolve** all spells and abilities you control on the stack
  2. **update** your game objects accordingly
  3. **announce** any updates needed to your opponent's game objects
* **hands off** - only pass clock once you've stopped touching game objects (except for cards in hand)

## explain it to me like im goldman sachs

* **102.1** - [...] The _active player_ is the player whose turn it is. The other players are _nonactive_ players.
* **117.3☀️** - If a non-active player would receive priority, instead they pass priority.
* **508.8.** - If no creatures are declared as attackers or put onto the battlefield attacking, skip the declare blockers and combat damage steps.

* **⏱️.1** - the active player passes clock once after the declare attackers step, and once after the cleanup step.
* **⏱️.2** - the defending player passes clock once after declaring blockers.
* **⏱️.3** - before a player passes clock, as well as before a player takes their first action after being passed clock, a player must first:
  * **⏱️.3a** - finish resolving all spells and abilities they control,
  * **⏱️.3b** - accurately represent the current game-state on their side of the table,
  *  **⏱️.3c** - announce any inaccuracy in the current game-state representation on their opponent's side of the table, and
  *  **⏱️.3d** - stop touching any game objects or counters, other than the cards in their hand


## how it *really* works

duct-taping priority to the active player lets you cut through steps and phases like butter. imagine the fluidity of choosing attackers in classic magic, but smeared across multiple phases here.

as the active player, you get to rapid-fire spells and abilities without fear of response, dropping cards onto the stack in bulk and resolving effects as fast as you like. as the non-active player, you get to sit back in empty awe, moving your pawns to the graveyard on command.

asynchronous action keeps play smooth. i know this can feel weird, but the order in which you and your opponent resolve effects and update the table only matters if it would impact the game state at two very specific snapshots in the turn - when blocks would be declared, and when you would untap for turn.

hands get clumsy, lands get half-tapped, triggers get missed. everyone makes mistakes, you just gotta fix em as they come up. a clear and accurate board-state is key!


## card choices

while you can probably sling blitz with MOM boosters, there are a couple mechanics that heck our fun premise, so! i've taken the liberty of weeding our card pool.

#### game-breaking cuts

* **19 cards that shuffle libraries** ([[Skittering Surveyor]], [[/Invasion of New Phyrexia]]) - shuffling wastes time, and idk who should pay time for this, so no one does.
* **25 cards that demand a choice of the non-active player** outside of blocking ([[Nezumi Informant]], [[Wary Thespian]]) - either directly, or via triggered abilities that can be triggered on either player's turn. these decisions stall the clock, which feels bad.
* **5 cards that allow determined players to break the format** ([[Render Inert]], [[Scrollshift]]) - either by defeating a non-active player's battle, or by causing a creature to enter under a non-active player's control.
* **[[Portent Tracker]]** - enables ugly clock-stalling tactics by untapping an opponent's land immediately before passing clock.

## the rules, nbd

i recommend best-of-5 matches, with a [5+2 time control](https://www.chess.com/terms/chess-time-controls) per game.

* start the clock after mulligans
* pass clock in 3 cases:
  * after ending your turn
  * after declaring your blocks
  * after moving to the "declare blockers" step, **IF** you're attacking **AND** the defending player has a legal block
* [getting flagged](https://www.chess.com/terms/flagging-chess) concedes the game
* **only the active player gets priority** &mdash; this means:
  * you can't cast spells or activate abilities on your opponent's turn.
  * on your turn, you don't need to pass priority or pause for response. just announce your actions and go!


## blitz etiquette

to tame the ultimately frantic late-game of blitz, let's set some ground rules before we start.

* **our game, our job** - we are collectively responsible for accurately tracking the game-state. this means announcing any corrections needed to our own or our opponent's stuff.
* **my stuff, my job** - i am responsible for physically moving & updating my stuff. that includes tokens, counters, tapping, milling, life total, etc.
* **my clock, my pace** - i won't wait while i'm on the clock! if i've announced any changes needed to your stuff, i can proceed as though it has been fixed.
* **clean as you go** - don't wait to tidy up! update your stuff as i play my turn. it saves you time when i pass the clock to you.
* **talk it out** - i will announce my actions, phases & triggers, just like in "real magic". passing priority is the one exception. heck priority! 🏴‍☠️
* **resolve-update-announce** (RUA /ruːˈæ/) - i will always complete these steps before passing the clock:
  1. **resolve** anything left on the stack
  2. **update** all my stuff
  3. **announce** any updates needed to your stuff
* **hands off the clock** - i will only pass the clock once i've stopped touching other game objects (except the cards in my hand). i won't touch the clock or hover over it otherwise, except to straighten it.

i gave these rules catchy names so you can scream them in the throes of battle. if they sound like staff training at a fast food joint, it's for the same dang reason (but please don't say "CAYGO" in this context - very confusing😅)

## tips

* invest in actual token cards - clarity is key! MOM has many different kinds of tokens.
* swap out DFCs during deckbuilding - i slip a [CARDNAME] note in front of a basic, and keep my DFCs with my tokens. easier than de-sleeving on the clock!
* don't wait for a response! this is a tricky pattern to unlearn & it drains valuable time
* when defending: pass clock before damage! another tricky pattern to unlearn, but can save you time if your opponent has effects or main phase 2 plays
* fliers, tricks, auras, attacking - all these get better
* board stalls are _hard_

## design notes

> above all else - magic deserves a visceral, kinesthetic counterpart. get players out of the tank and into their bodies. imperfection is fun!

years ago, i used to play magic with strangers online. the client had no rules, no animations, and players would lose connection or ghost all the time. to make the most of it, we learned to play  _**really darn fast**_.

while this compromised parts of magic, it also ripped me away from any sense of tilt, narrative, importance to a game. and with time, other patterns emerged. i love how magic feels when u play a lot rly fast, n i wanna share this with ppl who don't normally get to play this way.

_cutting priority out of magic_ &mdash; this might seem like a dramatic bastardization of magic's golden essence, but a turn of bare-bones, draw-go magic takes **at least 16 priority passes**, and you will literally break your phone before finishing a game. duct-taping priority to the active player lets you cut through steps and phases like butter.

imagine the fluidity of choosing attackers in classic magic, but smeared across multiple phases here. as the active player, you get to rapid-fire spells and abilities without fear of response, dropping cards onto the stack in bulk and resolving effects as fast as you like. as the non-active player, you get to sit back in empty awe, moving your pawns to the graveyard on command.

_this is a paper-first cube_ &mdash; i like the physicality of moving game-pieces around quickly, so i design with this in mind. i encourage you to try it out in paper, and i suspect the format will shine less online. but if you wanna try making it work online, chess does - so maybe you can too!

5MMM is my love letter to fast paper magic. the card list is just icing, so if u wanna DIY n play fast with a friend irl, any old pile will do, with a few tweaks.

> what we lose in strategic complexity, we make up for in ✨_speed_✨

_this cube concept was originally inspired by [100 thopters](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/100-ornithopters) and [the ball pit](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/ballpit). playtesting changes were also influenced by [bolt cube](https://cubecobra.com/cube/overview/bolt) and [blitz chess rules](https://www.hanleychessacademy.com/Blitz%20Chess%20Rules.pdf)._


## card choices

these cards break or sour the format - here's what i recommend weeding out.

#### game-breaking cuts

* **19 cards that shuffle libraries** ([[Skittering Surveyor]], [[/Invasion of New Phyrexia]]) - shuffling wastes time, and idk who should pay time for this, so no one does.
* **25 cards that demand a choice of the non-active player** outside of blocking ([[Nezumi Informant]], [[Wary Thespian]]) - either directly, or via triggered abilities that can be triggered on either player's turn. these decisions stall the clock, which feels bad.
* **5 cards that allow determined players to break the format** ([[Render Inert]], [[Scrollshift]]) - either by defeating a non-active player's battle, or by causing a creature to enter under a non-active player's control.
* **[[Portent Tracker]]** - enables ugly clock-stalling tactics by untapping an opponent's land immediately before passing clock.


#### watchlist

* **counter spells & protection**
  * some protection spells become drastically different ([[Saiba Cryptomancer]], [[Artistic Refusal]]), while others become much worse ([[Surge of Salvation]], [[Assimilate Essence]]). at least you can counter your own spells to pump your [[Gitaxian Spellstalker]]!
* **double-faced cards**
  * DFCs are frustratingly time-expensive if de-/re-sleeving is required. either use placeholders during matches, or cut these entirely.
* **cards that require the non-active player to reveal new information**
  * these reveal the opponent's hand - [[Traumatic Revelation]], [[/Invasion of Gobakhan]]
  * these cause the opponent to mill/exile cards you immediately interact with - [[/Etali, Primal Conqueror]], [[Breach the Multiverse]], [[Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer]], [[Gyruda, Doom of Depths]] (and occasionally, [[/Nezumi Freewheeler]])
  * these just cause the opponent to mill - [[Halo-Charged Skaab]], [[Tenured Oilcaster]]
  * all these cards can stall clock, to the caster's detriment (tho, the mill-only case rarely plays out this way)
  * ultimately, im keeping these all in for now to find out whether Etali/Breach are still as sweet as they were in original MOM. conceptually, i like the tension of needing an extra time buffer for these control wincons, but it could feel gross in practice. i need to know!
  * EDIT - Etali is still sweet. [[Traumatic Revelation]] is a time sap but still good on 2.
* [[Mirror-Shield Hoplite]] - 👀

* **102.1** - [...] The _active player_ is the player whose turn it is. The other players are _nonactive_ players.
* **117.3☀️** - If a non-active player would receive priority, instead they pass priority.
* **508.8.** - If no creatures are declared as attackers or put onto the battlefield attacking, skip the declare blockers and combat damage steps.

* **⏱️.1** - the active player passes clock once after the declare attackers step, and once after the cleanup step.
* **⏱️.2** - the defending player passes clock once after declaring blockers.
* **⏱️.3** - before a player passes clock, as well as before a player takes their first action after being passed clock, a player must first:
  * **⏱️.3a** - finish resolving all spells and abilities they control,
  * **⏱️.3b** - accurately represent the current game-state on their side of the table,
  *  **⏱️.3c** - announce any inaccuracy in the current game-state representation on their opponent's side of the table, and
  *  **⏱️.3d** - stop touching any game objects or counters, other than the cards in their hand
